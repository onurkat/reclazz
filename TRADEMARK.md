# Reclazz Trademark Policy

Reclazz is free and open source under the [Apache License 2.0](LICENSE).
The code is yours to use, modify, and redistribute.

The **name "Reclazz"** and the Reclazz logo are a different matter. As
[section 6 of the Apache License](LICENSE) makes explicit, the license
grants no trademark rights. This page explains what that means in
practice, in plain terms.

## What you can always do

- Use Reclazz, at work or at home, commercially or not, for free.
- Read, modify, and build the source.
- Fork the repository and publish your fork.
- Say truthfully that your project "is based on Reclazz", "is a fork of
  Reclazz", or "is compatible with Reclazz".
- Write about Reclazz, review it, teach it, quote it, screenshot it.

## What needs a different name

- Publishing a fork or a rebuilt copy to the JetBrains Marketplace, or
  anywhere else, **under the name "Reclazz"** or a name close enough to
  be confused with it.
- Using the Reclazz logo as your project's or product's logo.
- Naming a product, service, or company in a way that suggests it is the
  official Reclazz, or is endorsed by it.

Pick your own name for your fork. The code is the gift; the name is how
users know who maintains what they are running, and who to hold
responsible when a hot-reload goes wrong inside their server.

## Why this exists

Reclazz loads into the JVM of applications people depend on. If several
different builds circulate under the same name, a user who hits a
problem cannot tell whose build they are running, and neither can
whoever is trying to help them. The trademark keeps that answerable.

## Questions

Unsure whether your use is fine? Open an issue at
https://github.com/onurkat/reclazz/issues and ask. Reasonable uses are
usually granted, and asking costs nothing.
